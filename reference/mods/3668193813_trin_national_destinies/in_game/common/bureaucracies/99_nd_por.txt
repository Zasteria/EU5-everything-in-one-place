# Portugal (POR): bureaucracies
# National Destinies mod
#
# Read before choosing subjects (EU5 1.4):
#   vanilla bureaucracies Portugal can hold (bureaucracies/generic.txt, ten,
#     from Confessionalism or the age 4 advance): board of revenue, privy
#     council, audit bureau, colonial office, admiralty, foreign ministry,
#     trade commission, war council, ordnance board, commissariat. So no
#     Conselho da Fazenda, no Casa dos Contos, no Conselho de Estado and no
#     Conselho de Guerra here.
#   vanilla Portugal elsewhere: the Casa da India is a reform and a decision;
#     the Feitoria, the Ribeira das Naus and the Portuguese Quarters are
#     buildings; the Estudo Geral is a law; the foros are town rights; the
#     Almirante-Mor, the Ordenancas, the colonial governors and the Carreira
#     are advances; the Inquisition is a law every Catholic country can pass
#     (laws/00_religious.txt:80, inquisition_law), so no Santo Oficio.
#   this country's own tree (99_nd_por.txt): the Inquiricoes, the Cortes of
#     Coimbra, the keeper of the Torre do Tombo, the Order of Christ, the
#     Padroado, the cosmografo-mor, the capitaes-mores, the Junta do Comercio
#     and the Misericordia are advances or buildings.
#
# What is left, and what only this kingdom had in this form:
# 1. Juizes de Fora: the king's judge "from outside", sent into a town to
#    preside over its council.
# 2. Companhia das Naus: the register and common purse of 1380 that
#    replaced a lost ship at the cost of all shipowners.
# 3. Mesa da Consciencia e Ordens: the king's tribunal of conscience, which
#    ran the three military orders once their masterships were his.
# 4. Conselho Ultramarino: the council that read every letter from overseas
#    and advised on every post and reward there.
#
# Estate alignment differs in each: the crown against lords and town
# oligarchies, the shipowners against the nobles, the crown and the knights
# against the church, the nobility of service against the merchants.
#
# Slots. Vanilla gives Portugal none on the day it forms (a capital grants
# one only as a megalopolis, location_ranks/00_default.txt:50; Lisbon starts
# as a city). The auto modifier grants 2, half of these four. Vanilla adds
# one with the age 4 bureaucracy advance and one in age 6, and from
# Confessionalism its ten generic offices compete for the same slots.
#
# No cabinet seat and no character modifier. No key stands in both the funded
# and the neglected block of one institution. No government power key. All
# IDs are por_-prefixed (the last definition of a key wins across files).

# 1. Juizes de Fora
# Fetched (pt.wikipedia, "Juiz de fora"): created in 1327 under Afonso IV; a
# magistrate named by the crown who judged and also presided over the town
# council as a means of central control; consolidated by Joao III in 1532;
# more than fifty towns had one by 1580; ended with the constitutional regime.
# Fetched (pt.wikipedia, "Corregedor"): the corregedor of the comarca stood
# over the juizes de fora and the ordinary judges; in the lands of a lord
# there was no corregedor, an ouvidor named by the lord did the work.
# From memory, not fetched: that the towns paid the judge's salary and
# protested the cost in Cortes. That is why the burghers dislike the office;
# the lords dislike it for the fetched reason (the king's judge is the one
# their ouvidor keeps out).
# Urban control and not control at large: the judge sat in a town.
# Unpaid, a stranger with no kin in the town is lodged and fed by whoever has
# a house fit for him, and judges accordingly.
por_juizes_de_fora_bureaucracy = {
	implementation_price = price:implement_bureaucracy_price
	maintenance_price = price:maintain_bureaucracy_price
	removal_price = price:remove_bureaucracy_price
	maintenance_price_modifier = {
		value = country_economical_base
		multiply = 0.004
	}

	potential = {
		has_or_had_tag = POR
		has_game_rule = nd_bureaucracies_enabled
	}

	allow = {}

	estates_that_like = {
		crown_estate
	}
	estates_that_dislike = {
		nobles_estate
		burghers_estate
	}

	neutral_modifier = {
		monthly_towards_centralization = societal_value_monthly_move
	}

	positive_modifier = {
		scale = { value = scope:maintenance }
		global_monthly_urban_control = 0.002
		monthly_political_influence_gain_modifier = 0.05
	}

	negative_modifier = {
		scale = {
			value = 1
			subtract = scope:maintenance
		}
		global_nobles_estate_power = 0.2
		global_monthly_control_decline = 0.002
	}
}

# 2. Companhia das Naus (the key keeps the name of the Casa dos Vinte e
# Quatro, which stood here until 2026-10-02: saved games and the generated
# impact modifier hold the key. The house of the twenty four is told once,
# by the formation choice nd_por_casa_dos_vinte_e_quatro.)
# Fetched (pt.wikipedia, "Companhia das Naus"): founded in 1380 by Fernando
# I; every ship above fifty toneis was registered; two per cent of what a
# ship earned in freight went into a common fund; two purses, one at Lisbon
# and one at Porto; the fund paid owners for ships lost or damaged and for
# new hulls in their place, so that a loss was divided among all owners.
# From memory, not fetched: that Fernando's law of 1377 excused the owners
# of large ships from keeping horse and arms for the host. That is why the
# nobles dislike it: men who should ride with them are entered as shipowners.
# The gate is Lisbon: the first purse and the register were kept there.
# The country's age 1 building, the Casa da Bolsa, is the purse of 1293 that
# the merchants agreed among themselves; this is the king's company that
# made it compulsory ninety years later.
# Funded, a damaged ship is mended out of the purse. Unpaid, the purse is
# empty when a ship is lost: owners build under fifty tuns to stay off the
# register and keep their ships in the river.
por_casa_dos_vinte_e_quatro_bureaucracy = {
	implementation_price = price:implement_bureaucracy_price

	}

# 3. Mesa da Consciencia e Ordens
# Fetched (en.wikipedia, "Mesa da Consciencia e Ordens"): founded in December
# 1532 at Evora by Joao III; renamed in 1551, after a bull of Julius III,
# when the orders of Christ, Santiago and Avis came under it; it oversaw the
# University of Coimbra until 1772, the ransom of captives, the commanderies,
# and the goods of subjects who died outside the kingdom; abolished on 16
# August 1833.
# From memory, not fetched: that the 1551 bull joined the three masterships
# to the crown for ever, and that commanderies were the usual reward for
# service in Africa and India. The second is why the nobles like it.
# The gate reads vanilla's privilege clergy_military_orders (Portugal starts
# with it; it gives the clergy 25% power): the Mesa is what the crown does
# with orders it has, and a crown that has dissolved them has nothing to
# administer. The neutral line takes back part of that power.
# Unpaid, the commanderies are held by men who never see them and pay
# nothing on them, and the priests of the orders' churches go without.

		has_game_rule = nd_bureaucracies_enabled
	}

	allow = {
		has_estate_privilege = estate_privilege:clergy_military_orders

# 4. Conselho Ultramarino
# Fetched (pt.wikipedia, "Conselho Ultramarino"): created on 14 July 1642
# under Joao IV in place of the Conselho da India; a noble president and
# three councillors, two nobles and one jurist; seated on 2 December 1643
# under the Marquis of Montalvao; competent for India, Brazil, Maranhao,
# Guinea, Sao Tome, Cape Verde and the islands: finance, shipping, defence,
# the naming of officials and the granting of rewards; it shared money
# matters with the Conselho da Fazenda; dissolved on 30 August 1833.
# Vanilla's colonial office is about range and settlers. This council
# settled nobody: it answered governors and paid service with posts, so its
# lines are loyalty of the dominions and a contented nobility of service.
# One key is shared with vanilla's por_colonial_governors (subject_loyalty),
# the other key of that advance is left alone.
# From memory, not fetched: that the merchants and the Fazenda resented a
# council of soldiers deciding fleets and contracts. That is the dislike.
# The gate: a council for the overseas needs something overseas to answer.
# Unpaid, the consultas pile up, a governor waits two monsoons for an answer
# and in the meantime trades on his own account.
