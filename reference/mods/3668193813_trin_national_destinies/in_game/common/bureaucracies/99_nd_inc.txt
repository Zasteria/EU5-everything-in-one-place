# National Destinies: Tawantinsuyu (Inca Empire) bureaucracies
# Seven historically-grounded institutions of the Inca state, from the
# Pachacuti expansion (1438) through the Vilcabamba resistance (1572). Each
# bureaucracy reflects a distinct face of Inca governance: the Willaq Umu and
# the priesthood of the Sun, the four-suyu governorate, the quipu
# record-keepers, the mit'a rotational corvee, the mitma colonist transfers,
# the chosen-women's house, and the keepers of the road's bridges. On the
# day of forming the country's own grant gives 4 places: 4 of 7 institutions
# can run.

# 1. Willaq Umu and the Priesthood of the Sun: the high priest of Inti,
#    by custom a brother or uncle of the Sapa Inca, who held office for
#    life, named the priests of the Sun in the provinces and kept the
#    festival calendar. The lands and herds of the Sun in every province
#    fed the state feasts. The key keeps the older name: until 2026-10-02
#    this was the panaqa system, which vanilla already gives the Inca as
#    the panaqa building of its own government reform (has_panaqas).

# 3. Quipucamayoc Record-Keepers: the empire ran without writing on
#    knotted cords called quipus, whose plies, colors, knot-types, and
#    knot-positions encoded census, tax assignments, mit'a rotations,
#    and historical chronology. The Quipucamayoc were specialist
#    administrators trained in yachay wasi schools at Cuzco; only they
#    could read the cords. The system tracked the population of every
#    ayllu in the realm at decimal granularity and was the indispensable

# 4. Mit'a Rotational Labour Levy: every adult male of the conquered
#    ayllus owed the Sapa Inca a fixed rotation of labour each year on
#    state lands, in mines, on roads, in royal granaries, and in
#    military service. A separate caste of Yanakuna provided full-time
#    palace and elite servants outside the rotation. The mit'a
#    delivered the workforce that built Sacsayhuaman, Machu Picchu,
#    and the Qhapaq Nan, but it was an obligation owed by the
#    ayllu, not the individual, and grew brutal where the rotation

# 5. Mitma Resettlement System: when a region resisted incorporation
#    or held strategic value, the Inca state moved entire populations.
#    Loyal Quechua-speaking ayllus were transplanted into the conquered
#    territory and the conquered population, in turn, broken apart and
#    settled across the empire. The mitma simultaneously diluted local
#    cohesion, spread the imperial language, and gave rebellious zones
#    a counterweight of crown-loyal settlers. It was the most aggressive
#    instrument of Andean state-formation and it left a permanent

# 6. Acllahuasi: the House of the Chosen Women, where daughters of

#    hostage system (a curaca whose daughter served in the Acllahuasi
