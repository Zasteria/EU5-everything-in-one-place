# Valencia (VAL): bureaucracies
# National Destinies mod
#
# Read before choosing subjects (EU5 1.4):
#   vanilla bureaucracies Valencia can hold (bureaucracies/generic.txt, ten of
#     them, from Confessionalism or the age 4 advance): a board of revenue, a
#     privy council, an audit bureau, a colonial office, an admiralty, a
#     foreign ministry, a trade commission, a war council, an ordnance board
#     and a commissariat. None is repeated by name or by subject, so no
#     Mestre Racional (an auditor, Valencia's own from 1419) and no council.
#   vanilla names spa_council_of_aragon_bureaucracy for a release to come.
#   vanilla Valencia elsewhere: the Furs are a town right
#     (town_rights/15_iberia.txt:216), the lieutenancy an advance with a
#     building (so no viceroy), the Consulate of the Sea a law, the Germanies,
#     the silk guild and the Real Societat Economica its tag advances.
#   the mod's Catalonia (99_nd_cat.txt), Aragonese Empire (99_nd_cda.txt) and
#     Aragon (99_nd_ara.txt): the Consell de Cent, the Batllia General, the
#     vegueries, the Tribunal de Contrafaccions, the royal chancery, the
#     Generalitat, the Consolat de Mar, the Sommaria, the Real Audiencia and
#     three Aragonese courts. So no Generalitat of Valencia, no Batlle
#     General, no Audiencia of 1506, no Junta de Contrafurs, and no jurats
#     and Consell General (the same city government as the Consell de Cent).
#   this country's own tree (advances and building_types/99_nd_val.txt): the
#     Tribunal de les Aigues and its Comuna de Regants, Montesa, the aljames,
#     the Fabrica de Murs i Valls, the Centenar, the hospital, the coast
#     guard, the settlement charters, the Col.legi de la Seda, the Cort Ducal.
#     The Milicia Efectiva of 1597 is left out because the coast guard and
#     five units already cover the kingdom in arms.
#
# What is left are four offices that were Valencian first or Valencian in
# form, each with a different quarrel:
# 1. Mustassaf: the city's inspector of weights, prices and streets.
# 2. Pare d'Orfens: the judge who bound orphans and vagrants to masters.
# 3. Lligallo: the herders' assembly of the northern mountains.
# 4. Governacio: the king's governor and his lieutenants beyond the rivers.
#
# Estate alignment, on the road the files are written for (a Valencia
# released from Aragon or formed by a small Catalan monarchy: nobles, clergy,
# burghers and peasants all present): townsmen and common buyers against the
# clergy; masters against the poor; herders against the lords; the crown
# against lords and city alike.
#
# No government power key is granted: one road into the tag is a theocracy
# (Andorra).
#
# Slots. Vanilla gives Valencia none on the day it forms (a capital grants one
# only as a megalopolis, location_ranks/00_default.txt:50). The auto modifier
# grants 2, half of these four. Vanilla adds one with the age 4 bureaucracy
# advance and one in age 6, and from Confessionalism its ten generic offices
# compete for the same slots.
#
# No cabinet seat and no character modifier. No key stands in both the funded
# and the neglected block of one institution. All IDs are val_-prefixed (the
# last definition of a key wins across files).

# 1. Mustassaf
# The Christian heir of the muhtasib. The office appears in Valencia soon
# after the conquest of 1238, before any other town of the Crown had one;
# Barcelona had its own from 1339 (ca.wikipedia, "Mostassaf"). In Valencia he
# was chosen each year by lot among the citizens (es.wikipedia,
# "Instituciones del Reino de Valencia": "Mustacaf", insaculacion). He
# watched weights, measures and prices, the quality of food, the guilds'
# work, the cleanliness of streets and the building rules (ca.wikipedia).
# From memory, not fetched: the Llibre del Mustacaf of Valencia that other
# towns copied, the end of the office in Valencia with the decree of 1707,
# and that clerics disputed his right to fine them for what they sold, which
# is why the clergy is the estate that dislikes him.
# An office of the city of Valencia: it needs the city.
# Funded, bread is sold at the posted price and full weight, and the market
# is one the country round wants to come to. Unpaid, the inspector lives on
# what sellers give him to look away: false measures, and spoiled stores.
val_mustassaf_bureaucracy = {
	implementation_price = price:implement_bureaucracy_price
	maintenance_price = price:maintain_bureaucracy_price
	removal_price = price:remove_bureaucracy_price
	maintenance_price_modifier = {
		value = country_economical_base
		multiply = 0.004
	}

	potential = {
		has_or_had_tag = VAL
		has_game_rule = nd_bureaucracies_enabled
	}

	allow = {
		owns = location:valencia
	}

	estates_that_like = {
		burghers_estate
		peasants_estate
	}
	estates_that_dislike = {
		clergy_estate
	}

	neutral_modifier = {
		global_trade_center_power = 0.05
	}

	positive_modifier = {
		scale = { value = scope:maintenance }
		food_purchase_efficiency = 0.1
		global_non_rural_monthly_prosperity = 0.001
	}

	# Both are keys where a positive value is the harm.
	negative_modifier = {
		scale = {
			value = 1
			subtract = scope:maintenance
		}
		estate_enrichment = 0.2
		global_food_decay = 0.005
	}
}

# 2. Pare d'Orfens
# On 6 March 1338 Peter the Ceremonious, at the city's request, recognised a
# curator of orphans and of the destitute in Valencia; the name "pare dels
# orfens" followed within the century, and in 1407 Martin the Humane made
# him a judge of minors apart from the criminal justice. He placed orphans
# and abandoned youths with masters as apprentices and servants, inspected
# the contracts, and punished the idle with the whip and with expulsion.
# From 1518 he could hold a minor's wages until majority or marriage. The
# office spread to Castello (1386), Zaragoza (1475), Tudela (1527), Orihuela
# (1553) and beyond, became venal and hereditary, was discredited when a
# holder was convicted of fraud in 1760, and was suppressed by Charles IV in
# December 1793 (es.wikipedia, "Padre de huerfanos"; ca.wikipedia, "Pare
# d'orfes", gives 1337 and the pursuit of idlers and beggars).
# The masters were the guildsmen, so the funded side reads vanilla's guild
# privilege in the scale, as vanilla's Hisba reads its privilege
# (bureaucracies/muslim.txt:91-94). Sized for that case: 0.12 and 0.0015
# with formal guilds, 0.08 and 0.001 without.
# Funded, the workshops fill with bound apprentices who become artisans.
# Sold as a living, the office keeps the wages it holds and whips whoever
# complains, and the poor learn to stay away from the towns.
val_pare_d_orfens_bureaucracy = {
	implementation_price = price:implement_bureaucracy_price
	maintenance_price = price:maintain_bureaucracy_price
	removal_price = price:remove_bureaucracy_price
	maintenance_price_modifier = {
		value = country_economical_base
		multiply = 0.004
	}

	potential = {
		has_or_had_tag = VAL
		has_game_rule = nd_bureaucracies_enabled
	}

	allow = {}

	estates_that_like = {
		burghers_estate
	}
	estates_that_dislike = {
		peasants_estate
	}

	neutral_modifier = {
		global_pop_promotion_speed_modifier = 0.05
	}

	positive_modifier = {
		scale = {
			value = scope:maintenance
			if = {
				limit = { root ?= { has_estate_privilege = estate_privilege:formal_guilds } }
				multiply = 1.5
			}
		}
		global_burghers_city_desired_pop = 0.08
		burghers_estate_satisfaction_recovery = 0.001
	}

	negative_modifier = {
		scale = {
			value = 1
			subtract = scope:maintenance
		}
		peasants_estate_target_satisfaction = medium_permanent_target_satisfaction_penalty
		global_migration_attraction = -0.1
	}
}

# 3. Lligallo
# In 1271 James I allowed the men of Morella to hold a lligallo in their
# district ("possitis facere ligallum in termino Morelle"): the assembly of
# the herders, on a fixed day of the year, where every stray beast found in
# the district was brought and given back to its owner, the statutes were
# read and the officers chosen (es.wikipedia, "Ligallo"). The word and the
# custom spread over the north of the kingdom and the lower Ebro. From
# memory, not fetched: that Castello and other royal towns had their own,
# that the lligallo judged quarrels between herders and tillers over the
# drove roads, and that lords and the order of Montesa disputed the towns'
# herds their free passage and pasture, which is why the nobles dislike it.
# It is Morella's privilege: it needs Morella.
# No location of the kingdom has wool or livestock as its raw good, so both
# keys pay through sheep farms and feed the woollen mills of Alcoi in the
# country's own tree.
# An assembly once a year is a light office: upkeep 0.003, as vanilla's
# Byzantine set and the Bayt al-Mal.
# Unheld, nobody answers for a herd: it grazes the sown fields, strays are
# kept by whoever finds them, and the hill villages settle it themselves.

	implementation_price = price:implement_bureaucracy_price

		multiply = 0.003
	}

	potential = {
		has_or_had_tag = VAL
		has_game_rule = nd_bureaucracies_enabled
	}

	allow = {
		owns = location:morella
	}

	estates_that_like = {
		peasants_estate
	}
	estates_that_dislike = {
		nobles_estate

}

# 4. Governacio
# The king's governor in the kingdom, the portantveus de general governador,
# under the regime set by the reforms of 1344. Two governors: one in
# Valencia, from the Catalan border to Xixona, with lieutenants beyond the
# Uixo at Castello and beyond the Xuquer at Xativa, and one in Orihuela for
# the land beyond Xixona. Their powers were of government and above all of
# justice, and they could raise and lead a host; a governor had to be a
# native of the kingdom. From the fifteenth century they stood under the
# lieutenant general and from 1520 under the viceroy, and the Nueva Planta
# of 1707 replaced them with corregidores (ca.wikipedia, "Governacio de
# Valencia"; es.wikipedia, "Gobernaciones del Reino de Valencia" and
# "Instituciones del Reino de Valencia"). From memory, not fetched: that the
# governor's court took the cases of nobles and the truces of their feuds
# (the bandos of the Centelles and the Vilaragut around 1400), and that the
# jurats of Valencia fought his jurisdiction inside the city. Hence lords
# and townsmen both dislike him.
# This is not vanilla's lieutenancy (the viceroy, an advance and a building).
# Funded, a judge of the king sits within reach of every valley, and the
# south that was Murcian until 1304 is held to the kingdom. Unpaid, the
# governor is one more chief of a faction, with the king's seal.
val_governacio_bureaucracy = {
	implementation_price = price:implement_bureaucracy_price
	maintenance_price = price:maintain_bureaucracy_price
	removal_price = price:remove_bureaucracy_price
	maintenance_price_modifier = {
		value = country_economical_base
		multiply = 0.004
	}

	potential = {
		has_or_had_tag = VAL
		has_game_rule = nd_bureaucracies_enabled
	}

	allow = {}

	estates_that_like = {
		crown_estate
	}
	estates_that_dislike = {

	}
